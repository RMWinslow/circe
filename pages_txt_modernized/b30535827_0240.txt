But to change themselves substantially, and become another Man in all respects of Body and Mind; I believe not one in a Hundred wou'd comply with it; so few or rather none there are that look upon their Neighbor to be better than themselves; 'Tis no wonder therefore if I cou'd not perswade one of these Beasts to become Man again.— But ah! what a lovely young *Bullock* comes feeding towards me. How fierce one wou'd take him to be by his looks, yet how gentle and tractable he is. Indeed we are not a little obliged to Nature for making this Animal, since he takes so much drudgery and labour off our hands, and tho' he is of such prodigious strength, is so easie to be managed. I will ev'n satisfie my self whether the Man that was changed into him, was a *Grecian*, which I suppose may be so, since he stands still to listen to me, as if he understood me.—
