Dialogue III.

---

*Ulysses, Circe, the Hare.*

*Ulysses.* WERE not I fully convinced, Madam, of the Sincerity of your Affection to me, I should be apt to doubt whether you really intended to grant me the Favour I asked of you, and that being loath to deny it me, you put me upon such positive Wretches, that in Defiance of all Reason, were fully resolved not to become Men again, by which means I should be obliged to quit my design.

*Circe.* Harbour no such thoughts, my dear *Ulysses*, for such underhand dealing neither agrees with the love I bear you, nor with the Generosity of my Temper. But you know well enough, he that can't stand buff to a disappointment or two, does not deserve to succeed in any noble attempt.
