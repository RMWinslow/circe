---

Dialogue II.

---

*Circe, Ulysses, the Snake.*

*Circe.* Well, my dear *Ulysses*, and what do your Country-men the Grecians say to you? Are any of them willing to become men again?

*Ulysses.* Not one? 'Tis true indeed I have only discoursed with those two whom you show'd me, one of whom it seems had been a Fisherman in the days of yore, the other a Ploughman: so 'tis no wonder at all if two such sordid wretches, whose lives had been so miserable and painful, refused to try there fortune in the world again.

*Circe.* Don't think I did this without design. I resolved you should begin with those two fellows to convince you that even their humble condition, which has been so often and so extravagantly praised by many of your Writers, is attended by so many inconveniences, that the most contemptible and imperfect Beasts in the Universe enjoy a more happy
