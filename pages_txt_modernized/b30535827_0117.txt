*Ulysses.* And why so?

*Circe.* Because his Nature requires it. Tell me, I pray now, when a Stone falls, does it do so by force?

*Ulysses.* No, not in my opinion.

*Circe.* And yet it can't do otherwise than fall.

*Ulysses.* I own it, because Nature has ordained it so, and that Motion, which causeth it to descend towards the Centre, proceeding from an interiour Power, which is within the same, call'd Nature, must be natural to it and not violent, because all violent Motions are those that proceed from an exteriour Power. And therefore tho' the Stone can't act otherwise, yet it cannot be properly said to be forced.

*Circe.* Why so? Is it not drawn down by Force of its own Gravity?

*Ulysses.* Pardon me, Madam, not by Force, but by Nature, it being natural for a Stone to be ponderous, and if it were not so, it wou'd cease to be a Stone.
