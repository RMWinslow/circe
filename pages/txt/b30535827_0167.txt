*Ulysses.* What you say is very right.

*Lyon.* Physicians say, it is beneficial to be ill sometimes, Nature itself requiring it; but not such a sort of Sickness, that the Patient may be insensible of it, and not desire to be relieved, for to be desirous of a Cure, is half way towards it. This is quite contrary in the Evils of the Mind, for he that is infected with them, cannot make any right Judgment concerning himself, that very part that should do it, being affected. Upon this Account, Folly is the greatest Evil that can attend a Man, for he is ignorant of his Malady, and consequently never endeavours after a Cure.

*Ulysses.* This is the case of Drunkards, when the Wine has dilated those tender Parts where the interior Senses perform their Functions, who being ignorant of their Condition, are guilty of a thousand ridiculous Follies and Impertinencies.
