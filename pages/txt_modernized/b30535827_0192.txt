[178 — Circe, Ulysses, — Dial. VII.]

*Circe.* If these your wise Men of *Greece* are so few in number in respect of the rest, as you have told me they are, why then this Glory of yours would take up but a small compass, and consequently be of little value; for the multitude would not be able to estimate thy glorious Actions according to their merit, because they are not sensible how far Man is superior to a Beast.

*Ulysses.* No, no, you are mistaken. 'Tis infinitely better to be praised by only one Man, who is praised by a thousand, than by a thousand vulgar Sots, whose Names are not so much as known.

*Circe.* Since you are so ambitious of this Honour, How comes it about then that you have not succeeded in it all this while? What! have you not found so much as one yet that is willing to become Man again?

*Ulysses.* No, for all those little Wretches whom I hitherto conversed with, when they were Men, neither knew themselves nor their own value, but were wholly immersed in sensual Pleasure; and because in their present Condition they enjoy a few more Conveniences belonging to the preservation and welfare of their Body, than they did before, having no regard to the Divine and Celestial Part, they resolve to remain Beasts.

*Circe.*
