Now, both these things very often pluck back both you and us from that we should do, turning our Will and Appetite (which are the first movers of your Actions as well as ours) from that which they should seek after; either deterring them so by Fear, or alluring them with Pleasure.

*Ulysses.* What mean you, Friend, by all this heap of words?

*Horse.* Listen then, and I'll inform you. One of these two Impediments, call'd Fear, robs us of Fortitude, which Virtue reaches us to despise Fear, when we are engaged in any noble Enterprise. The other, which is Excess, robs us of Temperance, that suffers us not to take overmuch Delight in things that please, because they hurry us upon several things which we ought not to do. Both these things hinder us infinitely less than they do you, from those Operations that are convenient for us.
