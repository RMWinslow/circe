---
title: 1. The Oyster and the Mole
---




ULYSSES


OYSTER
Why what business have you with me, good master Ulysses?

I would call you by your own name too if I did but know it. 
But if you are a Grecian, as Circe informs me you are, prithee honest friend tell me so.

To satisfy your curiosity, know then that I was a Grecian before I was changed into an oyster, that I lived not far from Athens, that my name was Itacus, and that by occupation I was a poor fisherman.

Why then honest friend rejoice and be merry; For partly out of compassion, as knowing that you were born a man, and partly out of the love I bear thee as my country-man, I have prevailed with Circe to restore you to your human shape and deign to carry you home with me into Greece.

If this is all you have to say to me you might have saved yourself this trouble, for I must take the freedom to tell you, that all your fine rhetoric and eloquence, for which you're so famous among the Grecians, will make no impression upon me, and therefore it's but lost time to persuade me to leave all these conveniences, which in my present condition I so happily enjoy, without any perplexity or thought at all, in order to become man again, who is the most helpless unhappy creature in the universe.

To hear you talk that way, one would be apt to conclude that along with your human shape, you likewise lost your reason.

And you, Ulysses, will never lose yours because you have none, if you believe as you talk.
Putting raillery aside, let us argue this matter between ourselves and you shall see whether I, who have tried both conditions of man and beast, am able to maintain my point. 

Listen then, but before we proceed in our discourse, you must promise me beforehand, that while I open to speak, you will take care that those *rogue confounded crabs* shall not throw a stone between my two shells, for that would hinder me from shutting them ever after.
