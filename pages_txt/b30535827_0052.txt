*Ulysses.* Pray what are those Dainties we are so fond of, that were never meant by Nature for our sustenance and aliment?

*Snake.* What are they? Why t'wou'd be an endless labour to recount them to you: but to instance in some few, all those things you use by way of Sawces or Condiments, which tho' they are good to season other things, yet cannot be eaten alone, as Salt, Pepper, and the like.

*Ulysses.* Now for my part I was always of the contrary opinion; nay I have often heard it affirmed, that 'twas impossible for us to live without Salt.

*Snake.* The reason of that is, because you abound so in superfluous Humours, that are occasion'd by your excessive Eating and Drinking, and want something to dry them up. But if you fed upon simple Meats, and of these wou'd only eat so much as Nature requires, you wou'd not be troubled with any such inconveniences.
